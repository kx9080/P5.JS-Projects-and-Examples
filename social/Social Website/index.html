The Third Battle Of Ypres / The Battle of Passchendaele

One of the biggest battles in World War One, introducing an incredibly important technology to the war-front.

The Significance of the battle.

This battle was incredibly significant due to its use as a symbol of the senseless slaughter that occurred during the war. This battle did nothing for the Allied effort, and the estimated casualties ran up to 275,000 British, 220,000 Germans, and a known total of 15,654 Canadians. This battle was also the first major battle in which the Germans utilized chemical warfare.

The historical background of this battle.

Throughout the war, ships had been crucial in providing aid to the front lines. This was hampered by the German occupied ports in Belgium

The Location.

Near the village of Passchendaele, Belgium.

The Sides Involved.

The Allied Forces and the Nazis. Specifically, it was mostly the British and the Germans.

The Aftermath.

Little progress was made from this battle. They did not reach the ports, but they did take back the site of the village of Passchendaele.

The view of the battle as of now.

The general retrospective of it is that it was incredibly unsuccessful.
